## How to read this section

The matrix scores each risk on two independent axes: **likelihood** that it materialises for a typical Gulf NOC digital programme within three years, and **impact** (the severity of the consequence if it does). Score = likelihood × impact; bands are Critical (≥16), High (10–15), Medium (5–9), Low (≤4). Scores are my judgement from the evidence cited; the evidence is tagged <span class="tag success">certain</span> where a primary source was opened, <span class="tag">likely</span> where only secondary reporting exists, and <span class="tag danger">guessing</span> where the record is silent and I am filling the gap.

Three things the evidence forces, which a reader may not expect:

1. **<span class="tag success">certain</span> The demonstrated catastrophic risk in 2026 was kinetic, not cyber.** Between March and October 2026 drones and missiles hit Ras Tanura, Ras Laffan, KPC headquarters, Aramco's Dhahran headquarters, and two AWS cloud regions. No Gulf NOC disclosed an OT cyber disruption in the same period. A risk register that leads with ransomware is reading the 2019 threat landscape.
2. **<span class="tag success">certain</span> The most likely failure is not an attack at all.** About 70% of oil and gas companies never move past digital pilots (McKinsey), fewer than 20% of energy executives report AI scaled with measurable impact (Bain, 2026), and KOC's own 2018 uplift figure was published as "awaiting validation". Value non-realisation is the base case, not the tail.
3. **<span class="tag success">certain</span> Sovereignty is now a physical question.** Data-localisation rules kept backups inside the UAE and Bahrain; when those regions were struck, some localised data was lost permanently and cross-border emergency migration conflicted with the same rules. The law and the threat now point in opposite directions.

Reading the matrix: the top-right (Critical) corner holds one physical risk and two cyber risks that share a root cause — remote access into field systems by people and machines outside the NOC's perimeter. The dense middle band (score 12) is where programmes actually die: not from an attack but from unproven value, locked-in platforms, missing people and deferred budget. The bottom-right risks are near-certain but survivable; they are costs of doing business, not threats to the programme.

## What buyers now require, and what that implies for a vendor

These follow from the register; they are inferences <span class="tag">likely</span>, not sourced requirements unless marked.

| Driver | Emerging NOC requirement | Vendor implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R01, R04 | Operations continuity without the primary control centre or cloud region; in-country, multi-site redundancy; <span class="tag success">certain</span> ADNOC's sovereign-cloud RTOC is the template | Architecture that runs degraded on field servers; no single-region cloud dependency; documented failover drills |
| R02, R03, R17 | Vendor cybersecurity certification as a tender gate (<span class="tag success">certain</span> Aramco CCC with OT class; Kuwait NBCC by Oct 2027); IEC 62443 zones; brokered, logged remote access | Certification cost and lead time become bid-critical; remote-support model must be redesigned |
| R07, R08 | Outcome evidence, not dashboards: baseline, counterfactual decline, audited uplift | Contracts move from manpower-hours to measured outcomes; attribution methodology becomes a deliverable |
| R09, R10 | Data stays in-country and in the NOC's name; OSDU or equivalent open data layer; escrow and exit terms | Platform pitch must separate data layer from application layer; M&A continuity clauses |
| R06, R18 | Local staffing ratios written into the contract; knowledge transfer with named nationals | Delivery cost rises; subcontract to local manpower firms is the norm, not the exception |
| R11 | Timing and positioning that survives GNSS loss (holdover clocks, multi-constellation, inertial) | Spec change for 5G/SCADA timing and any drone or autonomous-rig scope |
| R13, R16 | Phased, cancellable scopes; price linked to oil-price bands; insurance-cost pass-through | Smaller tranches, longer terms, more options |
| R15 | Human-in-the-loop as default for autonomous control; explainability and audit trail per action | Autonomous features sold as advisory first; liability language in every AI scope |
