## The layered methodology stack

- The 2025–26 enterprise stack layers a **qualification / deal-management framework** (MEDDIC/MEDDPICC) over a **discovery method** (SPIN, Sandler Pain Funnel, Gap Selling) and a **value-messaging framework** (Command of the Message, ValueSelling, Challenger), with **JOLT** added for "no decision" ([Cold Calling Chronicles](https://coldcallingchronicles.substack.com/p/why-so-many-sales-methodologies-and)).
- Pick one or two per role; SDRs don't need enterprise frameworks ([Cold Calling Chronicles](https://coldcallingchronicles.substack.com/p/why-so-many-sales-methodologies-and)).

| Layer | Job | Frameworks |
|---|---|---|
| Qualification / deal management | Scorecard: is this deal real and winnable? | MEDDIC / MEDDPICC |
| Discovery | Find and quantify pain | SPIN, Sandler Pain Funnel, Gap Selling |
| Value messaging | Frame the value narrative | Command of the Message, ValueSelling, Challenger |
| Late-stage indecision | Beat "no decision" | JOLT |

- **Rep-free preference:** 61% of B2B buyers prefer a rep-free experience; 73% avoid suppliers sending irrelevant outreach; 69% see differences between a vendor's website and what sellers say; buyers want sellers for **"contextual judgment"** tasks such as deciding whether a product fits (Gartner, 632 buyers, 2024) ([Gartner, June 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience)).
- **Purchase regret:** 56% of organisations regret their largest tech purchase; 67% of tech-buying participants are outside IT; leading regret cause is unclear goals among the buying team; no-regret buyers did 4+ in-depth activities (implementation planning, scoping, trials) with the chosen provider only ([TechRepublic on Gartner](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/most-technology-purchases-come-with-high-degree-of-regret-gartner-says/)).
- **Committees:** average B2B purchase involves 13 stakeholders; ~89% of decisions cross departments (Forrester 2024 via GetAccept) ([GetAccept](https://www.getaccept.com/blog/mutual-action-plans)).
- **Procurement and trials:** procurement will play a key decision role in 60% of B2B deals in 2026 (53% in 2025); over half of buyers will rely on trials as a deciding step ([Digital Commerce 360 on Forrester](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/10/28/forrester-b2b-buyers-demand-proof-not-promises/)).

## Methodology reference cards

### MEDDPICC

Elements: **M**etrics, **E**conomic Buyer, **D**ecision Criteria, **D**ecision Process, **P**aper Process, **I**dentify Pain, **C**hampion, **C**ompetition ([Hyperbound MEDDPICC](https://www.hyperbound.ai/blog/meddpicc-discovery-questions)).

<details class="collapsible">
<summary>Metrics questions</summary>

- "What key metrics are crucial for your business success and how do you report on them?"
- "What metrics show you there is room for improvement in your current process?"
- "If we implemented our solution, what specific goals would you aim for and how would success be measured?"

</details>

<details class="collapsible">
<summary>Economic Buyer questions</summary>

- "Who has the final say in purchasing decisions like this, and will I be able to meet them as part of this process?"
- "What does success look like specifically for the economic buyer — what are their personal goals?"

</details>

<details class="collapsible">
<summary>Decision Criteria questions</summary>

- "Can you share the technical, financial and vendor criteria you'll use to evaluate solutions?"
- "Are the criteria weighted? What are the top 3 must-haves?"
- "Any intangibles — ease of implementation, vendor support — that will influence the decision?"

</details>

<details class="collapsible">
<summary>Decision Process questions</summary>

- "Outline the specific steps from today to a signed contract."
- "Besides the formal committee, who else needs to be consulted or approve?"
- "What's the timeline and are there key dates?"

</details>

<details class="collapsible">
<summary>Paper Process questions</summary>

- "What's the typical timeline for contract review and approval; who handles legal?"
- "Any legal, security or compliance checks (infosec reviews) we should anticipate now?"
- "What does the contract-change process look like?"

</details>

<details class="collapsible">
<summary>Identify Pain questions</summary>

- "What's the biggest challenge with [core process] and how is it affecting your business metrics?"
- "How have you tried to resolve it before and what happened?"
- "Who else is impacted and what are the downstream consequences?"

</details>

<details class="collapsible">
<summary>Champion questions</summary>

- "Who gains the most personally and professionally from our solution succeeding?"
- "Can you help me arrange a brief meeting with [Economic Buyer]?" — the champion test is whether they broker access.

</details>

<details class="collapsible">
<summary>Competition questions</summary>

- "Besides us, what other solutions or vendors are you considering?"
- "What do you like or dislike about the other options; how do we compare?"

</details>

**SPIN → MEDDPICC mapping** ([MEDDICC.com](https://meddicc.com/resources/spin-selling-vs-meddpicc-meddicc)):

| SPIN | MEDDPICC element |
|---|---|
| Situation / Problem | Pain |
| Implication | Pain + Metrics |
| Need-Payoff | Metrics + Decision Criteria |
| — (no SPIN equivalent) | Champion, Decision Process, Paper Process, Competition |

### SPIN

- **S**ituation, **P**roblem, **I**mplication ("especially powerful"), **N**eed-Payoff.
- Best for discovery calls when still identifying what's broken ([MEDDICC.com](https://meddicc.com/resources/spin-selling-vs-meddpicc-meddicc); [Cold Calling Chronicles](https://coldcallingchronicles.substack.com/p/why-so-many-sales-methodologies-and)).

### Challenger

"Teach, tailor, take control." Steps ([PandaDoc](https://www.pandadoc.com/blog/challenger-sales-process/)):

1. Warm up with credibility.
2. Reframe with a data-grounded angle.
3. Drive emotional impact of inaction.
4. Present value proposition as the answer.
5. Deliver the solution and propose next steps such as a pilot.

- Fits long cycles, crowded markets, indecisive committees.
- "Teach" can feel combative; pair with MEDDIC and SPIN.

### JOLT

<div class="kpi-grid">
<div class="kpi"><div class="eyebrow">Lost deals = no decision</div><div class="kpi-value num">40–60%</div><div class="kpi-note">indecision in ~9 of 10 calls <a href="https://challengerinc.com/losing-to-customer-indecision/">Challenger Inc.</a></div></div>
<div class="kpi"><div class="eyebrow">Win rate, high indecision</div><div class="kpi-value num">6%</div><div class="kpi-note">~30% of calls; medium indecision (~60%) wins 30% <a href="https://challengerinc.com/losing-to-customer-indecision/">Challenger Inc.</a></div></div>
<div class="kpi"><div class="eyebrow">Reps restart status-quo pitch</div><div class="kpi-value num">73%</div><div class="kpi-note">when cold feet appear; in 84% of those, fear/discounts made losing more likely <a href="https://challengerinc.com/losing-to-customer-indecision/">Challenger Inc.</a></div></div>
</div>

- 40–60% of lost deals end in no decision; indecision in ~9 of 10 calls; high-indecision calls (~30%) had 6% win rate, medium (~60%) 30%.
- **FOMU** (fear of messing up) beats FOMO.
- When cold feet appear 73% of reps restart the status-quo pitch, and in 84% of those cases adding fear/discounts made losing more likely ([Challenger Inc.](https://challengerinc.com/losing-to-customer-indecision/)).

Steps:

- **J**udge indecision — qualify on ability to decide.
- **O**ffer your recommendation — narrow options, right-sized purchase.
- **L**imit exploration — stop adding references once certainty plateaus.
- **T**ake risk off the table — realistic expectations, safety nets, accountability.

### Sandler

Seven stages: (1) rapport; (2) up-front contract (objective, agenda, outcome); (3) uncover pain; (4) budget; (5) decision process; (6) present; (7) post-sale reinforcement. **"No pain, no sale."** ([Hyperbound Sandler](https://www.hyperbound.ai/blog/sandler-pain-funnel))

<details class="collapsible">
<summary>Pain Funnel — surface questions</summary>

- "Tell me more about that."
- "How does your current process work?"
- "How long has this been happening?"
- "Can you give me a specific example?"
- "What have you tried so far to fix this?"
- "How has that worked out?"

</details>

<details class="collapsible">
<summary>Pain Funnel — business impact questions</summary>

- "What impact is this having on your team/department/company?"
- "How is this affecting your ability to meet your goals?"
- "What's the cost of this problem in time/money/resources?"
- "How does this compare to your competitors?"
- "What opportunities are you missing because of this?"
- "If you could solve this, what would it mean for your business?"

</details>

<details class="collapsible">
<summary>Pain Funnel — personal impact questions</summary>

- "How do you feel about that?"
- "What does this mean for you personally?"
- "How is this affecting your day-to-day work?"
- "What pressure does this put on you?"
- "Have you given up trying to solve this?"
- "If you don't address this, what happens to your role/team/career?"

</details>

### Gap Selling

- Close the gap between **current state** (environment, problem, impact, root cause, emotion) and **future state**.
- "Never sell to need — problems get you to the impact and the impact is where urgency, value, and need live."
- Discovery ≈ a quarter of the cycle; don't mention product during discovery; build a **Problem Identification Chart** before outreach ([Gong Gap Selling](https://www.gong.io/blog/gap-selling)).

| Question type | Example |
|---|---|
| Probing | "Can you tell me more about it?" |
| Process | "Walk me through what happens before/after [process]" |
| Provoking | "What impact does [problem] have on the rest of your team?" |
| Validating | "Am I understanding you correctly?" |

### Command of the Message

Value framework, in order ([Rework Command of the Message](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/pipeline-management/command-of-the-message)):

1. **Before Scenario**
2. **Negative Consequences** — "do nothing" is often the real competitor.
3. **After Scenario**
4. **Positive Business Outcomes** — ask "so what?" until it matters to an executive.
5. **Required Capabilities** — vendor-neutral first.
6. **Metrics** — baseline + target agreed before close.
7. **Differentiation** — no disparaging.
8. **Proof Points**

Required-capability test: do they set the minimum to get from before to after; are they compelling; is your differentiation explained through them?

**SE lever:** vendor-neutral required capabilities shape the buyer's RFP/decision criteria.

### ValueSelling

Qualified Prospect Formula as four questions ([ValueSelling](https://www.valueselling.com/resource-blog/how-to-identify-high-potential-opportunities-qualification-event-or-process)):

- Should they buy, and from you?
- Is it worth it?
- Can they buy (buying power, not title)?
- When will they buy?

Phases: Engage → Qualify → Advance → Close, with written mutual plans ([Highspot listing](https://www.highspot.com/marketplace/valueselling-valueselling-framework/)).

## When to use which

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| Scoring and forecasting every deal | MEDDPICC |
| Early discovery, still finding what's broken | SPIN |
| Uncovering business and personal pain | Sandler Pain Funnel |
| Quantifying current vs future state | Gap Selling |
| Long cycle, crowded market, indecisive committee | Challenger (paired with MEDDIC + SPIN) |
| Writing the value narrative and champion pitch | Command of the Message |
| Qualifying "should / worth / can / when" | ValueSelling |
| Late-stage stalls, cold feet, "no decision" | JOLT |

**Recommended stack for this persona** <span class="tag">inference</span>: MEDDPICC as scorecard; Sandler/Gap/SPIN questions to fill Pain and Metrics; Command of the Message to write the value narrative and champion pitch; JOLT for late stalls.
